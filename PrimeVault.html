Chapter One

Context & Problem

In early 2022, the DeFi ecosystem was simultaneously its most promising and most hostile moment for retail investors. Hundreds of yield protocols existed, yet the average person needed to understand liquidity pools, impermanent loss, slippage, gas auctions, private key custody, and smart contract risk, just to park five hundred dollars into a yield strategy. That is not democratised finance. That is a puzzle game with irreversible consequences.

I joined the founding team as Lead Product Manager to build PrimeVault, a protocol designed to function as a crypto-native equivalent of a mutual fund SIP. The product offered two algorithmically rebalanced vaults, each targeting a distinct risk appetite:

The core user problem was three-dimensional. First, cognitive overload: the sheer number of protocols, strategies, and tokens made informed decision-making impossible for a non-technical investor. Second, financial dread: gas fees routinely consumed 5-11% of small deposits, punishing the very users DeFi claimed to serve. Third, trust paralysis: a string of high-profile rug pulls and smart contract exploits had made every new protocol feel like a landmine. Users wanted systematic, compounding yield. Every tool required them to behave like a full-time quant trader.

I just want a crypto SIP. Deposit a fixed amount every month, let it compound, check it at the end of the year. But every time I try, I lose twenty dollars in gas before I even get in, I have no idea which pool is actually safe, and last week three people in my Telegram group got completely wiped out. I am terrified of doing something wrong and watching everything disappear silently overnight.
Composite user quote drawn from 14 discovery interviews, Q1 2023

That composite quote became our internal product north star. Every design decision, every prioritisation debate, every engineering trade-off was tested against one question: does this reduce that fear, or add to it?

On-Chain Analytics Insight That Changed Our Roadmap

I pulled on-chain data from Dune Analytics, specifically examining wallets that had interacted with Yearn Finance vaults over the prior 90 days, and cross-referenced their deposit sizes with their gas expenditure. The query structure was as follows:

Dune Analytics · "Gas Bleed on Small Depositors"
WITH yearn_deposits AS (
  SELECT evt_tx_hash, sender, amount / 1e6 AS deposit_usdc,
    gas_used * gas_price / 1e18 * eth_usd_price AS gas_cost_usd, block_time
  FROM yearn_ethereum.Vault_evt_Deposit yvd
  JOIN prices.usd p ON p.symbol = 'ETH' AND date_trunc('hour', p.minute) = date_trunc('hour', yvd.block_time)
  WHERE yvd.block_time >= now() - interval '90 days'
)
SELECT ROUND(deposit_usdc / 100) * 100 AS deposit_bucket_usd,
  AVG(gas_cost_usd) AS avg_gas_usd,
  AVG(gas_cost_usd / deposit_usdc * 100) AS gas_as_pct_of_deposit,
  COUNT(*) AS transaction_count, COUNT(DISTINCT sender) AS unique_wallets
FROM yearn_deposits
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY deposit_bucket_usd
Surprising On-Chain Finding

For wallets depositing under $500, the average gas cost represented 4.8% of the deposit value (approx.), spiking to 11% or more during peak congestion windows. This meant a first-time user depositing $300 was immediately down $14 to $33 before accruing a single dollar of yield. More critically, over 38% of wallets in this deposit bracket made zero subsequent on-chain interactions, suggesting they were effectively burned out of DeFi at their first point of contact. This single finding justified gas-batching infrastructure as a non-negotiable Day 1 platform feature, not a later optimisation.

Chapter Three

Product Definition & Core Experience

How the Algorithms Work, in Plain Language

Anchor Vault (Conservative) — Rebalancing Logic

The vault maintains a target allocation: 50% USDC deposited into Aave for stable yield, 30% stETH accruing Lido staking rewards, and 20% ETH held as a liquid reserve. An on-chain keeper bot checks allocation drift every week. If any asset moves more than 5% from its target weight, due to price movements or yield reinvestment, the rebalancer executes a single aggregated swap via 1inch to restore balance. The cost of this operation is bundled into the 0.15% annual management fee, charged on withdrawal. Users never see a separate rebalancing gas transaction.

Apex Vault (Aggressive) — Rebalancing Logic

This vault uses a momentum-weighted allocation across ETH, wBTC, and a curated basket of DeFi tokens. A Chainlink-fed oracle scores each asset on 30-day price momentum and protocol health signals: TVL growth rate, audit recency, and liquidity depth. Allocations shift when any asset drifts 8% from its score-weighted target, or bi-weekly regardless. The fee structure is 0.5% annual management fee plus a 10% performance fee applied only to gains above the 6-month high-water mark, protecting users from paying performance fees after a drawdown recovery.

The Hardest Prioritisation Decision

Three weeks before our planned public launch, the engineering team flagged they had capacity for one more major feature before go-live. I faced a genuine fork: build Option A, a structured educational onboarding module covering DeFi fundamentals and vault mechanics, or ship Option B, the conservative vault first with a polished but education-light experience, relying on product design to do the teaching.

I ran a RICE scoring exercise with the full cross-functional team to make the trade-off visible rather than political:

The RICE score confirmed my instinct: ship the conservative vault first, and treat the product design itself as the education. A clean risk quiz, progressive disclosure, and the live risk score visualisation would do more for user confidence than a five-screen onboarding tutorial that most users would skip. The formal education module shipped six weeks post-launch, built on real user data about exactly which concepts generated the most support tickets.

Cross-Functional Team Alignment

The team was a 12-person squad spanning 3 Solidity engineers, 2 frontend engineers, 1 tokenomics designer, 1 security researcher, 1 data engineer, and 3 in growth and marketing, distributed across four time zones. Alignment required a deliberate operating structure:

  • 01
    Joint Spec Format: I created a shared specification document that included the user story and the contract interface side by side. Solidity devs and frontend engineers had historically worked from separate documents, causing late-stage "this is not buildable" surprises. The joint format meant engineering constraints surfaced in planning, not in review.
  • 02
    Tokenomics Red Team Sessions: Every two weeks, the tokenomics designer and I ran an adversarial review of the fee and incentive structures, stress-testing against whale extraction scenarios and liquidity withdrawal spirals. Two incentive designs were blocked before shipping because this process surfaced perverse dynamics before they became live exploits.
  • 03
    Growth as Research Partners: I embedded the growth lead in every user interview from week one. By the time we wrote landing page copy, the marketing team had heard users' exact language around fear and trust. Our launch landing page converted at 8.3% (approx.) wallet connection rate, versus a DeFi industry benchmark of approximately 3-4%.

Analytics, Experimentation & Growth

The Dune Dashboard I Owned

I built and maintained the team's primary analytics dashboard in Dune from day one. Three query sets formed the core of our weekly data reviews:

Query 1 · TVL by Vault Over Time
SELECT date_trunc('day', block_time) AS day, vault_type,
  SUM(net_deposit_usd) OVER (PARTITION BY vault_type ORDER BY date_trunc('day', block_time)) AS cumulative_tvl_usd
FROM primevault.vault_events
WHERE event_type IN ('Deposit', 'Withdraw')
ORDER BY day, vault_type
Query 2 · 30-Day Retention Cohorts
WITH first_deposit AS (
  SELECT wallet, MIN(block_time) AS cohort_date
  FROM primevault.vault_events WHERE event_type = 'Deposit' GROUP BY wallet
),
still_active AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT e.wallet
  FROM primevault.vault_events e
  JOIN first_deposit fd ON e.wallet = fd.wallet
  WHERE e.block_time BETWEEN fd.cohort_date + interval '25 days' AND fd.cohort_date + interval '35 days'
)
SELECT date_trunc('week', fd.cohort_date) AS cohort_week, COUNT(fd.wallet) AS depositors,
  COUNT(sa.wallet) AS retained_30d,
  ROUND(COUNT(sa.wallet) * 100.0 / COUNT(fd.wallet), 1) AS retention_pct
FROM first_deposit fd
LEFT JOIN still_active sa ON fd.wallet = sa.wallet
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY cohort_week
Query 3 · Referral Attribution by Wallet
SELECT referrer_wallet,
  COUNT(DISTINCT referred_wallet) AS referrals_driven,
  SUM(referred_deposit_usd) AS referred_tvl_usd,
  AVG(referred_deposit_usd) AS avg_referred_deposit,
  MIN(referral_timestamp) AS first_referral_date
FROM primevault.referral_events
GROUP BY referrer_wallet
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT referred_wallet) >= 2
ORDER BY referred_tvl_usd DESC

Chapter Seven

Key Learnings & Transferable Insights

What I Would Do Differently

  • 01
    Audit the aggressive vault in parallel, not sequentially. I correctly prioritised the conservative vault for our first third-party security audit. But the aggressive vault launched with only an internal review, and we discovered a minor reentrancy edge case in month four that required an emergency migration. A concurrent external audit, even a lighter-touch one, would have caught it at a fraction of the reputational cost. Security reviews are not a feature to be scored on a RICE matrix; they are load-bearing infrastructure.
  • 02
    Invest in withdrawal UX as carefully as deposit UX. We allocated roughly 80% of our UX research and iteration effort to the deposit flow. When the bear market deepened and users wanted to exit, the withdrawal experience was notably worse by comparison. Retention data showed that users who experienced a friction-heavy withdrawal were 40% less likely to re-deposit within 90 days, even when market conditions improved. A smooth exit builds re-deposit trust.
  • 03
    Instrument intent, not only action. Our Dune dashboard told us precisely what users did on-chain, but it could not tell us where they hesitated, raged-clicked, or quietly abandoned a screen before connecting their wallet. I would invest in a session analytics layer for dApps from day one, integrating something like Hotjar or a custom event stream from the wallet interaction layer, to capture friction points that on-chain data cannot see.
  • 04
    Ship governance infrastructure alongside token distribution. We distributed PRIME governance tokens as liquidity mining incentives in month two but did not ship a governance interface until month six. Token holders felt unheard and disempowered, creating community friction at precisely the moment we needed goodwill. A minimal on-chain voting interface should have shipped the same week as the token.

Connecting DeFi Product Management to Traditional Fintech

Building PrimeVault was operationally equivalent to designing a mutual fund SIP feature on a platform like Groww or Zerodha, with one additional layer of technical complexity. In both contexts, the PM's core job is identical: abstract a financial instrument behind a clean user decision, price the product to balance user value and business sustainability, instrument cohort retention to identify which users are compounding their relationship versus quietly disengaging, and manage a recurring investment experience that compounds trust over time. The DeFi version adds smart contract risk management, but conceptually this maps directly to settlement risk in traditional finance. Just as a payments PM on a UPI or RTGS system must define what happens when a transaction is confirmed on one leg but fails on the other, a DeFi PM must define what happens when a vault transaction is submitted to the mempool but fails due to insufficient gas, or when a keeper bot misses a rebalance trigger during a volatile price event. In both cases the PM is not writing the settlement or contract logic, but they own the failure mode definitions, the user communication for those failures, the retry and recovery paths, and the escalation triggers. The week I managed a failed rebalance event on Apex Vault and wrote the user communication within two hours, making sure no wallet was left with an ambiguous state and no user felt they had been silently abandoned, was structurally identical to a fintech PM handling a failed NACH mandate reversal or a delayed NEFT batch. The protocol stack is different; the product discipline is the same, and it transfers directly.

Smart Contract Risk is Settlement Risk

In traditional finance, settlement risk is the danger that one leg of a bilateral transaction completes while the counterpart leg fails, leaving a party with an unintended exposure. In DeFi, a failed atomic transaction during a rebalance can carry the same structural risk profile: the vault may have successfully exited Position A but not yet entered Position B, leaving the portfolio in a temporarily imbalanced and over-exposed state. Managing this required me to write formal fallback specifications for oracle failures, define keeper bot redundancy thresholds, and design circuit-breaker conditions with the same rigour a fintech PM applies to a T+1 batch settlement fallback. The vocabulary is different. The discipline of defining edge cases, owning the failure specification, and protecting users from silent errors is identical, and that is precisely the muscle I bring to any financial infrastructure product role.
