Key Funnel Insight

The steepest drop occurs between the Study Space tile tap and the map view loading (a 45-point drop in session share), and again within the map itself before a space is selected. Combined, these two steps account for 80% of total session loss. The map view was identified as the primary UX failure point, not the check-in mechanism itself.

SQL: Segmenting Drop-Off by Department and Time of Day

myUoB Telemetry DB · Study Space Funnel Segmentation
WITH sessions AS (
  SELECT
    session_id,
    user_id,
    department,
    device_type,
    date_trunc('hour', event_time)          AS event_hour,
    EXTRACT(hour FROM event_time)            AS hour_of_day,
    event_name,
    event_time
  FROM app_events.myuob_events
  WHERE event_time BETWEEN '2024-05-01' AND '2024-06-15'
    AND event_name IN ('study_space_tile_tap','map_view_load','space_selected','checkin_complete')
),
funnel AS (
  SELECT
    user_id, department, device_type,
    CASE WHEN hour_of_day BETWEEN 8  AND 11 THEN 'Morning (8-12)'
         WHEN hour_of_day BETWEEN 12 AND 14 THEN 'Lunchtime (12-15)'
         ELSE 'Afternoon/Evening' END AS time_band,
    MAX(CASE WHEN event_name = 'study_space_tile_tap' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS tapped,
    MAX(CASE WHEN event_name = 'map_view_load'        THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS viewed_map,
    MAX(CASE WHEN event_name = 'space_selected'       THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS selected,
    MAX(CASE WHEN event_name = 'checkin_complete'     THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS checked_in
  FROM sessions
  GROUP BY 1,2,3,4
)
SELECT department, time_band, device_type, COUNT(*) AS sessions,
  ROUND(AVG(viewed_map) * 100, 1) AS map_view_rate_pct,
  ROUND(AVG(checked_in) * 100, 1) AS checkin_rate_pct,
  ROUND(AVG(checked_in) / NULLIF(AVG(tapped),0) * 100, 1) AS tile_to_checkin_pct
FROM funnel
GROUP BY 1,2,3
ORDER BY checkin_rate_pct DESC
-- Used to identify which departments and time windows have highest/lowest conversion

✨ Product Leadership Recommendation

The next high-value investment is calendar app integration for automatic space booking. Power users already plan study sessions in Google Calendar or iCal. If the app detects a study block in a synced calendar, it can pre-book the user's historically preferred space 30 minutes before the session begins and send a confirmation notification. This transforms the feature from reactive search into proactive ambient support, the product equivalent of how Google Maps pre-loads a commute route before you open the app. Projected impact: 35-45% reduction in peak-time "no spaces found" abandonment and measurable uplift in library utilisation rate during off-peak hours through predictive load distribution.

"Module Registration & Waitlist" –
Where Students Think They Are Done When They Are Not

Core QuestionHow might we eliminate the false-completion problem on the waitlist confirmation step and reduce registration-related support tickets by 40%?

Primary Funnel Insight

The waitlist confirmation step loses 40% of sessions in under 8 seconds (measured via session replay). Students who reach this step frequently exhibit "false completion" behaviour: they close the modal assuming they are now registered, when in reality they are on a waitlist with uncertain status. This is a modal design and copy failure, not a backend problem.

Surprising Mixpanel Finding

PGT students in large lecture-format modules (150+ enrolled) showed a 54% waitlist bounce rate, the highest of any segment. Filtering Mixpanel events by the prerequisite_warning_shown event revealed that 71% of PGT bounces occurred within 3 seconds of a prerequisite warning appearing, suggesting students read the warning as a hard block rather than an advisory note, and exited without attempting to proceed.

Hotjar Session Replay Analysis: UX Friction Patterns

  • 01
    Modal Dismissal Pattern: In 68% of waitlist-bounce sessions, students tapped the modal's X button within 4 seconds of it appearing. The modal was displaying "You have been added to the waitlist for COMP-302" with an OK button. Students interpreted tapping OK as completing registration, then closed the app. The system state was waitlisted; the student believed state was enrolled.
  • 02
    Rage Click Cluster on Prerequisite Warning: Heatmap data showed a dense rage-click cluster on the "View Prerequisites" link in the warning modal. The link opened a PDF that required browser sign-in, creating a dead-end. Students who hit this dead-end had a 78% session abandonment rate vs. 31% for those who did not trigger the prerequisite warning.
  • 03
    Back-Button Loop: 23% of sessions in the "Confirm Registration" step showed a back-navigate event followed immediately by a forward-navigate, repeated 2-3 times. This is a strong signal of uncertainty: students do not understand whether confirming the screen commits them, so they review and re-review before committing. The confirm button had no secondary label explaining its consequence.

"I clicked OK on the waitlist thing and thought I was registered. Found out two weeks later I was not in the module when the reading list came out and I was not on it. Nobody told me."

Direct quote, UG Year 2, Engineering · Support ticket #4,821, October 2024

Design Recommendation: Progress Indicator and Notification Opt-In

The proposed design change addresses both failure modes: the false-completion problem and the notification gap. Rather than a dismissible modal, the waitlist state is surfaced as a persistent progress stepper visible in the module card, with three explicit states:

  • 1
    Browsing – Module in basket, not yet actioned.
  • 2
    On Waitlist – Request submitted. Current position in queue shown (e.g., "Position 4 of 8"). Distinct amber status colour. Inline prompt: "Notify me when a place becomes available?" with one-tap push notification opt-in.
  • 3
    Registered – Confirmed. Green status. Module appears in timetable with no further action required.

Prerequisite warnings are replaced with an inline expandable card that shows: "This module recommends X. You can still apply, and your personal tutor will review." This removes the dead-end PDF link and replaces it with a reassuring, actionable message.

✨ Business Impact Projection

Registration-related support tickets cost an estimated 14 advisor-hours per enrolment week across the student services team. A 40% ticket reduction frees approximately 5-6 advisor-hours per week during peak enrolment periods, which translates to more capacity for complex queries that require human judgment. The push notification feature also creates a re-engagement mechanism for students who might otherwise miss their waitlist clearance window, reducing the "unclaimed places" problem where cleared spots go unfilled because students do not check their email promptly.

Analysis C · Feature Validation & Retention Impact

"Personalised Timetable with Calendar Sync" –
Does One-Tap Sync Drive Meaningful Retention?

Core QuestionDo students who sync their myUoB timetable to a native calendar show meaningfully higher weekly active days, and does a redesigned one-tap sync nudge drive adoption?

Exploratory Analysis: Sync vs. Non-Sync Retention Comparison

The myUoB app offers a calendar export via an .ics URL, but the adoption rate was 11.4% of active users as of Semester 1 2024, measured by the calendar_sync_activated event. I ran a quasi-experimental retention analysis comparing 30-day behaviour of sync adopters versus non-adopters, using propensity score matching to control for selection bias (students who voluntarily sync are likely already more engaged).

30-Day Weekly Active Days: Sync vs. Non-Sync Cohorts (PSM-adjusted, n = 800 matched pairs)

Non-sync users (matched control)
Retention Insight

After propensity score matching on year of study, department, and baseline engagement level, synced users averaged 2.8 weekly active days in the 30-day observation window vs. 1.9 days for non-sync users, a +47% difference. This survived the PSM adjustment, suggesting the relationship is not entirely explained by pre-existing engagement differences, though it is directional rather than causal in the absence of a true randomised experiment.

The low adoption rate (11.4% overall) despite the large retention signal is the core product opportunity. The current sync flow requires students to: tap Timetable → tap Share → tap Export .ics → open Files app → open with Calendar → subscribe. That is five steps across two apps. Most students do not complete this flow and never discover the benefit.

Mini PRD: One-Tap Timetable Sync Feature

User Story · PRD Excerpt · Timetable SyncMYUOB-US-14 · Sprint 1 Priority

Feature: One-Tap Timetable Sync with Native Calendar & Permission Nudge

As a first-year student who has just received their confirmed timetable in myUoB,

I want to add all my lectures, seminars, and assessments to my phone calendar with a single tap,

So that I receive native calendar reminders, can plan my week without opening the app, and do not miss sessions due to forgetting to check myUoB.

  • AC1 After timetable confirmation, a nudge card appears on the app home screen: "Your timetable is ready. Add to Calendar for reminders?" with two CTAs: "Add Now" and "Not Now." Displayed once per semester, not on every session.
  • AC2 Tapping "Add Now" triggers the native iOS Calendar / Android Calendar permission request. If granted, all confirmed sessions for the current academic term are written to a dedicated "myUoB Timetable" calendar within 2 seconds, with no further steps required.
  • AC3 Each calendar event includes: module code and title, room and building, lecturer name (where available), and a deeplink back to the module page in myUoB.
  • AC4 If the student selects "Not Now," the option is accessible permanently under Profile → Calendar. A secondary nudge (in-app banner, not push) appears 7 days later if the student has not synced.
  • AC5 Timetable changes (room swaps, cancellations) propagate to the synced calendar within 15 minutes via a background calendar refresh. Student receives an in-app notification of the change.
  • AC6 Analytics events fired: sync_nudge_shown, sync_nudge_accepted, sync_nudge_dismissed, sync_completed, sync_error.

Before/After Rollout Comparison Plan

If a true A/B test is not feasible within the release cycle, I would use a pre/post cohort comparison with a 4-week pre-launch baseline (Semester 1 weeks 2-5) and a 4-week post-launch observation window (Semester 2 weeks 2-5). Primary comparison: sync adoption rate (11.4% expected pre-launch vs. target 30%+ post-launch) and 7-day WAD for students who onboard at the start of each semester. This controls for seasonal effects by comparing equivalent semester-start windows across academic years.

✨ Business Case for Investment

At 11.4% current sync adoption, myUoB is leaving its highest-retention mechanism underutilised at scale. If the one-tap nudge moves adoption from 11.4% to 30% across a student body of 35,000 active app users, that is approximately 6,500 additional students entering the high-retention sync cohort. If the 47% WAD improvement observed in the exploratory analysis holds, this cohort will generate significantly more in-app sessions, increasing the surface area for every other feature and engagement initiative. The engineering cost is modest (native CalDAV/CalKit integration, estimated 6-8 sprint points on iOS and Android). The strategic value is disproportionate: calendar sync acts as a daily behavioural anchor for the app, making myUoB a persistent part of a student's weekly planning rhythm rather than a tool they visit reactively.
